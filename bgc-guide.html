Getting around BGC

Transport

Unlike Makati, jeepneys and tricycles are banned inside BGC itself — the BGC Bus is the only public transport allowed within the district, and it's cashless only (Beep card or GCash; a one-time ₱15 paper QR ticket is available at the two main terminals). Streets are almost entirely one-way, so missing your stop means riding the full loop (~20–30 min) rather than crossing back.

Grab is the easiest way to get around for anyone unfamiliar with the routes — fare and route are shown upfront, no need to learn the bus loops or jeepney signage. It's the default recommendation for visitors, especially at night or with luggage.

Grab App

Transport

Southeast Asia's main ride-hailing app — also handles food delivery (GrabFood) and groceries (GrabMart). Free to download, pay by cash or card in-app. Worth installing before you land.

Drinking Water

Health

Stick to bottled or filtered water. Metro Manila's municipal supply is treated to national standards at the source, but quality after that depends on each building's own pipes and storage tanks — which varies and is outside anyone's control as a visitor. Bottled water is cheap and available everywhere (convenience stores, hotels, restaurants); most hotel rooms also provide a couple of complimentary bottles daily.

From NAIA Airport

Transport

BGC sits roughly 8km / ~15–20 minutes from NAIA Terminal 3 in light traffic — one of the shorter airport-to-district hops in Metro Manila. That said, travel time is traffic-dependent more than distance-dependent: the same trip can stretch past an hour during rush hour (7–10am, 5–9pm) or in rain. Grab or the airport taxi queue are the simplest options; avoid Fridays if you have a flexible arrival time.

Walkability

Layout

BGC is one of Metro Manila's most walkable districts — wide sidewalks, greenery, and a 1.6km linear park (the "Greenway") along the western edge near the golf course. Most of what's on this page sits within a comfortable walking radius of Bonifacio High Street.

Burgos Circle

Neighborhood

Forbes Town Center · ~650m from High Street

A separate dining/nightlife strip within BGC, not a single venue — an outdoor circle of restaurants, bars, and cafes around a small park, generally more relaxed than the Fort Strip club scene. Daytime is quiet (mostly office workers from nearby JP Morgan, Sun Life); it picks up after dark, especially weekends. Kenshin Japanese Izakaya (on the Restaurants tab) sits right on its edge, along Forbestown Road.
